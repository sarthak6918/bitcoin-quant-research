BTC/USDT Spot vs Perpetual Futures

Mean, volatility, autocorrelation & volume clustering — 2019-01-01 to 2026-07-29 · Binance public data
Source & method. Spot and USDT-M perpetual futures (BTCUSDT) OHLCV fetched directly from Binance's public REST APIs — no key required, real historical data, not synthetic. Perp futures launched 2019-09-08, so perp series start there; spot covers the full 2019-01-01 start. Daily bars (2,767 spot / 2,517 perp) for the headline stats below; hourly bars (66,174 spot / 60,368 perp) confirm every finding at finer granularity. Reproducible via fetch_data.py + analyze.py in this folder.

1 — Price & the spot–perp basis

Over the full overlap period (2019-09-08 onward, n=2,517 days), spot and perp daily closes correlate at 0.99982 — they are, for return purposes, the same underlying price series. The mean absolute basis (perp vs. spot) is 0.049%, spiking to a maximum of 1.80% during the highest-stress days (funding-rate extremes, liquidation cascades). This is expected: perpetual futures are engineered via funding rates to track spot tightly.

Both series show strong negative skew and very heavy tails (excess kurtosis 23-57, vs. 0 for a normal distribution) — large down-moves are both more frequent and more extreme than a Gaussian model would predict, in both markets equally.

3 — Autocorrelation of returns (random-walk check)

Lag-1 return autocorrelation is small and negative in both series — spot -0.070, perp -0.069 (daily); spot -0.017, perp -0.015 (hourly). This is the classic microstructure signature of bid-ask bounce / mean-reverting noise at short lags, present almost identically in both markets. A Ljung-Box test across the first 10 lags rejects the pure-white-noise null for both series (Q≈30-33 vs. a 95% critical value of 18.3 at 10 df) — there is a small, statistically detectable linear structure in raw returns, but it is small in magnitude and, per the rest of this project's findings, not economically exploitable at these magnitudes once real trading costs are included.

4 — Volatility clustering (the ARCH effect)

This is the more important autocorrelation result. While raw returns show almost no persistence, the magnitude of returns (|return|) is strongly autocorrelated: lag-1 ACF of 0.165 (spot) / 0.160 (perp) daily, and 0.284 / 0.293 hourly — far larger than the return autocorrelation itself, and decaying slowly across all 10 lags rather than cutting off. Squared returns show the same pattern (ACF 0.091 / 0.093 daily). Ljung-Box on |returns| is enormous in both series (Q up to ~29,650 hourly) — this is unambiguous, textbook volatility clustering: calm periods and turbulent periods each persist, in both spot and perp, to essentially the same degree. This is the empirical basis for GARCH-family volatility models and is why volatility (not direction) is the more tractable forecasting target in this data — consistent with everything else this project has found about direction prediction.

Perp futures trade ~4.75× the volume of spot on average (311K vs 66K BTC/day) — expected, since leveraged derivatives concentrate speculative and hedging flow more than the underlying. Perp volume is also proportionally less variable (CV 0.72 vs 1.22) — spot volume swings harder in relative terms, spiking around specific news/liquidation events, while perp volume stays persistently high as a base level of derivatives activity.

The clearest structural trend: volatility clustering strengthens markedly in 2025-2026 (|return| ACF rising to 0.18-0.23, vs. near-zero in 2021), alongside declining absolute daily volume — a maturing, lower-turnover market where moves cluster more once they start.

Bottom line

Spot and perpetual futures are statistically near-identical in returns, volatility, and autocorrelation structure — correlation 0.9998, matching mean/vol/skew/kurtosis within noise. The one genuine structural difference is volume: perp trades ~4.75× spot's volume with more stable relative turnover. The most exploitable statistical regularity in this data is not return predictability (weak, and consistent with this project's other findings) but volatility clustering — both markets show strong, slow-decaying persistence in |returns|, identically, which is the standard justification for GARCH-style volatility forecasting rather than direct price-direction modeling.
